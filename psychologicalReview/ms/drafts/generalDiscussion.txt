Unlike the previous simulations, OM10 could not be applied to the current target finding. The reason for this is that the model cannot meaningfully represent the critical manipulation in Liu and Spelke’s (2017) experiments. This manipulation relates to the fact that differently sized barriers were present during both the habituation and test phases of the experiments reported in that paper. In contrast, barriers are absent during the prediction (i.e., test) phase in OM10 irrespective of the condition to which networks are assigned. This feature of the model—which here is treated as a theoretical commitment—means that OM10 can neither simulate Liu and Spelke’s (2017) experiments nor represent graded differences in infants’ putative sensitivity to action efficiency. Although it would be possible to modify OM10 such that obstacle information remained present during the test phase, doing so would constitute a substantial departure from how the model was originally conceived by Overwalle (2010) and from how it was implemented in the previous simulations. In other words, such a modification would transform OM10 into a fundamentally different model.
