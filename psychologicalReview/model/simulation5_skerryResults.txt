At first glance, this result may appear to be at odds with the finding that proportionally infants in Experiment 2 looked at both test events for an equivalent amount of time. This may not be the entire story. This is because inspection of the Supplementary Materials revealed that numerically infants looked longer at the Efficient test event than at the Inefficient one, although this difference fell just short of the significance threshold. It is possible that this difference would have reached statistical significance if a larger sample of infants were tested; each experiment reported in Skerry et al. (2013) tested a mere 20 infants. These results can help to clarify the performance of networks in the Effective Action condition. If network’s behavior in this condition stemmed from their participation in an action phase prior to the habituation phase, then networks in the Effective Action condition and those in the Ineffective Action condition should have responded in an equivalent manner to the two test events. The fact that their behavior did differ indicates that networks’ (and perhaps by extension, pre-reaching infants’) performance in the Effective Action condition was driven by the attention-heightening properties of effective action experience.
